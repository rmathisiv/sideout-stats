How it works

Ratings, win probabilities, and how we check our work — for the 2026 season.

The rating

Every team gets a single number, Sideout Rating, on an Elo-style scale. Every team starts each season at 1500; teams carried over from last year keep half of their previous rating. Beating a strong opponent moves the number up more than beating a weak one, and the size of the move also reflects how many sets the winner dropped and the point margin.

A ~100-point gap at a neutral site means the favorite wins about 64% of the time. 200 points is a heavy favorite; 50 points is close to a toss-up.

Win probability

Each matchup's win chance comes straight from the two ratings and the venue:

P = 1 / (1 + 10-(Rhome + 65 - Raway) / 400)

The +65 is the home-court edge, in rating points — roughly worth 8 points of win probability for an even matchup. At a neutral site, or for tournament/championship games, the +65 drops to 0.

Predicted set scores

The model's most likely scoreline (3-0, 3-1, 3-2, 2-3, 1-3, or 0-3) is derived from the same win probability. We solve for the per-set win chance that would produce that match probability over a best-of-5, then pick the scoreline the set-by-set math says is most probable.

It's the single most likely score — not the only possible one. Upsets and five-setters happen exactly as often as the probabilities say they should.

Brier score

The Brier score measures how accurate our probabilities are, not just whether we picked the winner. For each game we compare our predicted win chance to what actually happened; perfect predictions score 0, and flipping a coin (always predicting 50%) scores 0.25. Lower is better.

Our backtests land around 0.19 — well better than a coin flip, and competitive with published ratings systems in other sports.

Backtest results

Two out-of-sample tests, re-run on 2026-09-30 over 7,420 completed games (5,131 in 2025, 2,289 in 2026 to date):

2025 NCAA tournament · n=63 68.3% correct Brier 0.191
Trained only on the 2025 regular season, then predicted all 63 NCAA championship games — no tournament data was used in training.

2026 walk-forward · n=2,288 71.6% correct Brier 0.187
Each 2026 game predicted with only the games before it, then the result folded in — the closest simulation of live, day-by-day forecasting.

Data & limitations

The model is trained on official box scores pulled from a public NCAA data API (ncaa-api.henrygd.me) — match results, sets, and per-set point totals. That's all it sees.

What it does not see: injuries, lineup changes, TV/broadcast information, travel schedules, or anything off the court. Ratings also need a few matches to stabilize — early-season numbers (roughly the first month) are noisier, since new teams start at 1500 and returning teams keep only half of last year's rating. Treat September ratings as provisional.
